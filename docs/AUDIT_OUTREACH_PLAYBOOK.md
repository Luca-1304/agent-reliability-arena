# AI Agent Reliability Audit — Outreach Playbook

## Positioning

Offer one fixed-scope review of a tool-using workflow. The value is not “more AI”; it is separating a completion claim from independently observable proof before the workflow is trusted with consequential actions.

## High-fit prospects

Prioritise organisations already building or deploying:

- email, CRM, ticketing or document agents;
- browser or desktop automations;
- research and data-collection workflows;
- customer onboarding or support automations;
- internal operations agents;
- multi-agent orchestration;
- AI consultancy client solutions.

Best contacts:

- founder or technical co-founder;
- head of AI/product/engineering;
- automation lead;
- AI consultant or agency owner;
- operations leader responsible for the workflow.

Avoid generic support inboxes unless the company is genuinely small and no responsible contact can be identified.

## Qualification signals

Strong signals:

- the workflow changes external state;
- a false success could waste money, damage trust or create manual cleanup;
- the team has traces, fixtures or staging access;
- the workflow is bounded enough to review;
- the owner can approve scope and access.

Weak signals:

- only a chatbot with no external action;
- no available evidence;
- request for unrestricted production access;
- expectation of a security/compliance certificate;
- vague “make all our AI reliable” scope.

## Contact structure

1. Name the specific workflow or product feature.
2. Identify one plausible reliability question without pretending a defect is proven.
3. Explain the completion-evidence distinction in one sentence.
4. Offer a bounded fit review for one workflow.
5. Link the public evidence and service page.

Do not send a generic essay. Personalisation should be factual and short.

## Discovery questions

- What external state is the workflow meant to change?
- What currently tells the system it is finished?
- What evidence exists outside the acting agent's own report?
- What retry, rollback or partial-failure cases have appeared?
- Can representative or redacted traces be shared?
- Who owns approval for access, spend and any later implementation?

## Outreach hygiene

Track:

- organisation and contact;
- exact workflow/relevance;
- source and date verified;
- message sent;
- reply status;
- next action and due date;
- duplicate-contact check;
- reason for rejection or no-fit.

Do not contact the same person or organisation repeatedly without a reason. Verify addresses and use the organisation's appropriate hiring, founder or technical route.

## First-engagement boundary

- one workflow;
- fixed deliverables;
- fixed price after fit review;
- representative/redacted evidence preferred;
- no production mutation during discovery;
- implementation quoted separately;
- provider/tool spend separately capped;
- no guarantee, certification or penetration-test claim.

## Learning loop

After each 20-prospect cycle, record:

- replies and qualified conversations;
- language that created or reduced interest;
- recurring objections;
- workflows most frequently mentioned;
- missing evidence or credibility asset;
- whether the offer, audience or channel should change.

Do not treat silence as proof the service has no value; treat it as evidence about targeting, message, channel or timing and change one variable deliberately.
